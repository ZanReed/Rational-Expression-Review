SECTION 1 — EXPONENTIAL GROWTH
When a quantity increases by a fixed percent each period
Exponential growth occurs when a quantity exponentially increases by a fixed percent each year (or other time period). Formula:   \(f(t) = a(1+r)^t\)
  • \(a\) = initial amount
  • \(r\) = growth rate (as a decimal)
  • \(t\) = time
1IDENTIFY the initial amount \(a\) and the time \(t\).
2CONVERT the percent rate to a decimal (e.g., 7% → 0.07).
3SUBSTITUTE into \(f(t)=a(1+r)^t\).
4EVALUATE with a calculator and round as directed.

SECTION 3 — COMPOUND INTEREST
Interest calculated on principal AND accrued interest
Compound interest occurs when interest is calculated on both the principal amount AND the accrued interest thus far. Formula:   \(A = P\!\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{n}\right)^{nt}\)
  • \(P\) = principal (initial amount)
  • \(r\) = interest rate (as a decimal)
  • \(n\) = number of times compounded per year
  • \(t\) = time in years
Common values of \(n\): annually = 1, semi-annually = 2, quarterly = 4, bimonthly = 6, monthly = 12, weekly = 52, daily = 365.
1IDENTIFY \(P\), \(r\) (as decimal), \(n\), and \(t\).
2SUBSTITUTE into \(A = P(1 + r/n)^{nt}\).
3EVALUATE — use parentheses carefully on your calculator.
4READ THE QUESTION: “balance/value” means \(A\); “interest earned” means \(A - P\).

SECTION 4 — MIXED PRACTICE & CONTINUOUS GROWTH
Choose the right model — growth, decay, compound, or natural base \(e\)
Continuous growth or decay uses the natural base \(e \approx 2.71828\): \(f(t) = ae^{kt}\) — growth when \(k>0\), decay when \(k<0\). On your calculator, use the ex button.
Quick reference — which formula?
  • Increases by X% each year → growth: \(a(1+r)^t\)
  • Decreases by X% each year → decay: \(a(1-r)^t\)
  • Compounded n times per year → compound: \(P(1+r/n)^{nt}\)
  • Function given as \(f(t)=ae^{kt}\) → substitute and evaluate
